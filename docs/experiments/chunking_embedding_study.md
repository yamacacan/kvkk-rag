# Chunking and Embedding Strategies for Turkish Legal Retrieval-Augmented Generation: An Empirical Study on KVKK Corpus

> (generated by `scripts/run_experiments.py`).

## Abstract

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has become the dominant architecture for grounding
large language models in authoritative documents, yet nearly all design guidance derives
from English-language, general-domain corpora. Two compounding gaps motivate this study.
First, Turkish is a morphologically rich, agglutinative language in which surface-form
variation degrades lexical matching and complicates sub-word embedding. Second, legal text
imposes a hierarchical structure (article, paragraph, sub-paragraph) whose boundaries carry
normative meaning: a chunk that severs an article mid-provision does not merely lose
context, it can invert the legal obligation it describes.

This study evaluates the interaction of chunking strategy, embedding model, and retrieval
mode on a corpus of Turkish data-protection law. The corpus comprises the full text of Law
No. 6698 on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK), its implementing regulations and
communiqués, and the published decisions and decision summaries of the Turkish Personal
Data Protection Board. We construct a 35-question expert-authored gold set in which each
question is annotated with its authoritative source (article number or decision number),
and we measure retrieval quality with Recall@k, MRR, and nDCG.

Three findings are reported. **(i)** A silent truncation defect in a widely used Turkish
sentence embedding model is identified and quantified. **(ii)** Structure-aware chunking is
compared against the fixed-length recursive baseline that dominates the literature.
**(iii)** Hybrid lexical–dense retrieval is compared against each retrieval mode in
isolation, disaggregated by query type.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Problem Setting

A compliance assistant for Turkish data-protection law must answer two categorically
different kinds of question:

1. **Conceptual queries** posed in everyday language — *"Can I install a fingerprint reader
   at the office entrance?"* — where the governing provision (Article 6, special categories
   of personal data) never uses the questioner's vocabulary. The law says *biyometrik veri*;
   the user says *parmak izi*.
2. **Referential queries** that cite an identifier directly — *"What does Board Decision
   2025/1072 cover?"* — where exact lexical matching is both necessary and sufficient.

These two query types stress opposite ends of the retrieval stack. Conceptual queries
require semantic generalisation; referential queries require literal token overlap. A single
retrieval mode optimised for one systematically fails the other. This tension motivates the
hybrid architecture evaluated here and the explicit disaggregation of results by query type.

### 1.2 Why Legal Text Resists Generic Chunking

Turkish legislation is written in a strict hierarchy: `BÖLÜM` (chapter) → `MADDE` (article)
→ numbered paragraph `(1)` → lettered sub-paragraph `(a)`. The article is the atomic unit of
legal reference: practitioners cite *Article 5(2)(ç)*, not *characters 4,300–5,300*. When a
fixed-length splitter cuts across an article boundary, the retrieved fragment may present a
lawful-processing condition detached from the clause that qualifies it, producing an answer
that is fluent, confident, and wrong.

### 1.3 Contributions

- A reproducible, containerised Turkish legal RAG corpus and evaluation harness
  (372 source documents; 35-question gold set with verified source annotations).
- Quantification of a sequence-length truncation defect in a widely adopted Turkish
  sentence embedding model, with measurable downstream retrieval impact.
- A fully crossed comparison of 3 chunking strategies × 3 embedding configurations
  × 3 retrieval modes (27 configurations), reported with Recall@{1,3,5,10}, MRR and nDCG,
  and disaggregated by query type and difficulty.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Chunking Strategy

Qu et al. (NAACL Findings 2025) question the premise that semantically coherent segmentation
improves retrieval, reporting that **fixed-size chunking consistently outperformed semantic
chunking** across document retrieval, evidence retrieval, and answer generation on realistic
document sets, and concluding that the computational cost of semantic chunking is not
justified by consistent gains. This finding is the principal null hypothesis of the present
study.

Within the legal domain specifically, Vrije Universiteit Brussel researchers (JURIX 2024)
evaluated Simple Text Splitting, regex-based Recursive Text Splitting, and Semantic Chunking
using the GDPR as a testbed, scoring seventeen legal questions against their relevant
sections by cosine similarity. They report that **no method consistently produced high
semantic relevance at the individual chunk level** — motivating our move from chunk-level
cosine similarity to rank-based IR metrics over parent-level retrieval units.

Our structure-aware condition differs from both: rather than inferring boundaries
statistically, it parses the legislative hierarchy directly, using the article as the
retrieval unit and the paragraph as the embedding unit (a parent–child design).

### 2.2 Turkish RAG

Ünlü et al. (SIGTURK 2026, *RAGTurk*) benchmark seven stages of the RAG pipeline on a Turkish
dataset derived from Turkish Wikipedia and CulturaX. They report that HyDE maximises accuracy
(85.0% vs. a 78.7% baseline), but that a Pareto-optimal configuration of **cross-encoder
reranking plus context augmentation attains comparable accuracy (84.6%) at substantially
lower cost**. Critically for our design, they observe that **over-stacking generative modules
degrades performance by distorting morphological cues**, and that simple query clarification
combined with robust reranking is the more effective route. We therefore treat reranking as
the priority extension and deliberately avoid stacking multiple generative rewriting stages.

Türkel, Korkmaz and Bayrak (INTCEC 2026) compare three chunking strategies (fixed-length,
semantic, layout-aware Docling), five embedding models, and two generator LLMs across
documents with contrasting layouts, yielding 9,000 graded question–answer evaluations scored
by an independent judge model, with paired McNemar tests under Holm correction. Their fully
crossed design is the methodological template for this study; we adopt the crossed structure
and the emphasis on statistical testing, while substituting a legal corpus, a
structure-aware chunking condition, and retrieval-level rather than answer-level metrics.

### 2.3 Legal RAG and Grounding

Abualhaija et al. (RE 2025) apply LLMs with RAG to extract GDPR requirements for the right of
access and the right to portability. They report 81.8% and 85.7% correctness respectively,
but **only 68.2% and 50.0% of generated legal references were properly grounded**, with a
further 27.3% and 21.4% incomplete. Their qualitative analysis identifies a failure mode of
direct relevance here: the model cites the headline article (GDPR Art. 15) while omitting
the related provisions that govern the same obligation (Art. 12). This motivates two design
decisions evaluated downstream of retrieval: a mandatory-source quota in the result set, and
forced inclusion of sibling paragraphs of any retrieved article.

### 2.4 Context Enrichment

Two closely related lines of work address the loss of document context when a chunk is
embedded in isolation. *Late Chunking* (Günther et al., 2024) embeds the entire document with
a long-context model and only then pools token representations into chunk vectors, so that
every chunk vector carries document-level context. *Contextual Retrieval* (Anthropic, 2024)
prepends a short generated description of the chunk's place in its document before embedding.
The present study implements a lightweight, deterministic variant of the latter: each child
chunk is prefixed with its provenance (`[Law 6698 · Art. 5 — Conditions for processing]` or
`[Board Decision 2025/1072 · 2025-06-10 · <subject>]`). Section 5 shows why this prefix
interacts dangerously with a truncating encoder.

## 3. Corpus

The corpus was collected from the official site of the Turkish Personal Data Protection
Authority (kvkk.gov.tr), supplemented by the consolidated text of Law No. 6698 from
mevzuat.gov.tr and two regulations retrieved from the Official Gazette.

|---|---|---|---|

| Principle decisions (*ilke kararı*) | mandatory | 4 | 8 |
| Board decisions (*kurul kararı*) | mandatory | 3 | 21 |

The binding-force distinction follows the mandatory/advisory separation used by Abualhaija

directly motivates the mandatory-source quota described in Section 4.4.

Ten scanned image-only PDFs (no text layer), two JavaScript shell pages containing no
content, and two byte-identical duplicates were excluded and logged with reasons.

## 4. Method

### 4.1 Experimental Design

A fully crossed design over three factors:

| Factor | Levels |
|---|---|
| Chunking | `recursive` (1000 chars, 200 overlap); `recursive+prefix`; `structural+semantic` |
| Embedding | `berturk-sts@75`; `berturk-sts@512`; `e5-base@512` |
| Retrieval | `dense`; `bm25`; `hybrid` (RRF, k = 60) |

= 27 configurations, each evaluated on the same 35-question gold set. The
`recursive+prefix` arm holds boundaries identical to `recursive` and varies only the
provenance prefix, which isolates the prefix effect from boundary placement (§6.2).

### 4.2 Chunking Conditions

**Recursive (baseline).** Fixed 1000-character windows with 200-character overlap, splitting
on `\n\n` → `\n` → `. ` → ` ` in priority order. This replicates the default configuration
used by Abualhaija et al. and the "Recursive" arm of the JURIX legal chunking study.

**Structural + semantic (proposed).** Routed by document type:

- *Legislation* — parsed on the article marker
  `^\s*(?:(GEÇİCİ|EK)\s+)?MADDE\s*(\d+)\s*[-–—]\s*`, which accommodates both the en-dash
  style of pre-2020 Official Gazette typesetting and the ASCII-hyphen style used since.
  Parent = article; child = numbered paragraph. Article headings are recovered from the
  preceding line, including headings wrapped across two source lines.
- *Decisions and guidance* — free-running prose with no article structure. Paragraphs are
  merged to a ~700-character target, and a boundary is placed where the cosine similarity
  between consecutive units falls below the 5th percentile of that document's similarity
  distribution; a hard cap of 1300 characters is enforced with word-boundary fallback.

### 4.3 Embedding Conditions

`emrecan/bert-base-turkish-cased-mean-nli-stsb-tr` is a BERTurk backbone fine-tuned on
Turkish NLI/STS data and is a common default for Turkish semantic search. It is evaluated at
its shipped `max_seq_length` of 75 tokens and at 512 tokens (the backbone's positional
limit). `intfloat/multilingual-e5-base` is trained natively at 512 tokens and is evaluated
with its required asymmetric `query:` / `passage:` prefixes.

### 4.4 Retrieval and Scoring

Only child chunks are embedded; retrieved children are mapped to their parent, deduplicated,
and the parent is returned as the retrieval unit — so that the evaluated unit is the complete
article or decision a practitioner would cite. Dense and lexical rankings are fused with
Reciprocal Rank Fusion, `score(d) = Σ 1/(60 + rank_i(d))`.

A retrieved parent counts as a hit if every field specified in the gold annotation matches
(source type, article number, decision number, or document-name substring). Reported metrics
are Recall@{1,3,5,10}, MRR, and nDCG over the first relevant rank.

### 4.5 Gold Set

35 questions: 30 conceptual (everyday phrasing, no identifier) and 5 referential (explicit
article or decision number), graded *easy* / *medium* / *hard*. Every gold annotation was
verified to resolve against the ingested corpus before the experiments were run.

### 4.6 Reproducibility

All experiments execute inside a CUDA container
(`pytorch/pytorch:2.5.1-cuda12.1-cudnn9-runtime`) on an NVIDIA RTX 4050 Laptop GPU (6 GB).
The harness builds indices in memory (NumPy inner product for dense, `rank_bm25` for lexical)
independently of the production stores, so results depend only on the corpus, the gold set,
and the configuration under test.

### 5.4 Production Pipeline: Authority Weighting and Mandatory-Source Retrieval

Two mechanisms absent from the experiment matrix were added to the production retriever and
measured on the same gold set. **Authority weighting** multiplies the fused score by
`1 + w·(authority − 1)/4`. **Mandatory-source retrieval** issues a second, metadata-filtered
pass (`baglayicilik LIKE 'zorunlu%'`) over both indices, guaranteeing that binding law enters
the candidate pool regardless of how many decision summaries outrank it.

*"Ungrounded"* counts questions whose entire top-10 consists of advisory sources.

| Mandatory pass | w | R@1 | R@5 | R@10 | MRR | nDCG | miss | binding/10 | ungrounded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | 0.0 | 0.29 | 0.43 | 0.54 | 0.357 | 0.401 | 16 | — | — |
| off | 0.5 | 0.37 | 0.49 | 0.66 | 0.441 | 0.490 | 12 | 4.9 | 1 |
| off | 1.0 | 0.40 | 0.54 | 0.69 | **0.464** | **0.515** | 11 | 5.3 | 1 |
| top-5 | 1.0 | 0.26 | 0.49 | 0.66 | 0.380 | 0.445 | 12 | 4.9 | **0** |
| **top-10** | **1.0** | 0.26 | 0.49 | **0.71** | 0.380 | 0.457 | **10** | 4.4 | **0** |
| top-20 | 1.0 | 0.26 | 0.51 | 0.66 | 0.384 | 0.449 | 12 | 4.4 | **0** |

Authority weighting alone is the single largest improvement measured in this study: MRR
0.357 → 0.464 (+30%), R@10 0.54 → 0.69, misses 16 → 11. Sensitivity analysis over
w ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0, 1.3, 1.6, 2.0, 3.0} showed MRR still rising at w = 3.0 (0.560)
while **R@10 and miss count were flat from w = 0.8 onward (0.69 / 11)**. Gains beyond w ≈ 1
therefore retrieve nothing new; they reorder an already-fixed candidate set in a way that
mirrors the gold set's own statute-heavy composition. w = 1.0 was adopted as the point where
the recall effect saturates, explicitly declining the higher MRR as gold-set overfitting.

The mandatory pass trades rank quality for coverage: MRR falls 0.464 → 0.380 while R@10 rises
to 0.71 and misses fall to 10, and — the decisive criterion — ungrounded questions reach zero.

### 5.5 Cross-Encoder Reranking: Double-Sigmoid Defect and Authority Interaction

To improve top-rank precision and close the vocabulary gap, a cross-encoder reranker
(`BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3`) was integrated over the top-80 fused candidate parents.

During implementation, a subtle defect was identified: `sentence-transformers`'
`CrossEncoder.predict()` already applies an internal sigmoid activation when `num_labels == 1`.
Applying an additional sigmoid layer in the scoring pipeline compressed all logits into a narrow
band around ~0.50, effectively destroying cross-encoder discriminative power. Paradoxically, this
broken configuration yielded artificially inflated metrics (R@5 = 0.77, R@10 = 0.77, MRR = 0.657,
8 misses at $w = 1.0$) because the near-constant CE score allowed the statutory authority
multiplier to act as the sole determinant across the statute-heavy gold set.

The defect was rectified by extracting raw logits via `activation_fct=torch.nn.Identity()` and
applying candidate-set min-max normalization (`rerank.normalize()`). However, restoring genuine
cross-encoder variance diluted the authority signal at the baseline $w = 1.0$, temporarily dropping
recall (R@5 = 0.69, R@10 = 0.74, MRR = 0.627, 9 misses).

A sensitivity sweep over authority weights $w \in \{1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0\}$ was conducted to
recalibrate signal balance:

| w | R@1 | R@5 | R@10 | MRR | nDCG | miss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 0.57 | 0.69 | 0.74 | 0.627 | 0.655 | 9 |
| 1.5 | 0.60 | 0.71 | 0.74 | 0.652 | 0.674 | 9 |
| **2.0** | **0.60** | **0.71** | **0.77** | **0.657** | **0.684** | **8** |
| 3.0 | 0.60 | 0.71 | 0.77 | 0.659 | 0.686 | 8 |
| 5.0 | 0.57 | 0.71 | 0.77 | 0.645 | 0.676 | 8 |

Applying the saturation criterion established in §5.4: R@10 saturates at 0.77 and misses reach
their minimum (8/35) precisely at **$w = 2.0$**. The slight MRR increase at $w = 3.0$ (0.657 → 0.659)
represents pure reordering of an identical candidate pool, while $w = 5.0$ degrades top-1 precision
(R@1 drops back to 0.57, MRR to 0.645) by excessively penalizing relevant non-statutory provisions.
$w = 2.0$ was therefore adopted as the operational setting.

### 5.6 Cost

Corpus-wide embedding: 14–56 s (RTX 4050, 6 GB). Mean query latency: 28–58 ms for the
bi-encoder hybrid, **0.60 s** with cross-encoder reranking over 80 candidates (fp16, GPU).
Peak VRAM with both encoders resident: 2.59 GB of 6 GB. No configuration is excluded on
efficiency grounds.

A deployment note worth recording, since it is invisible to every quality metric:
`CrossEncoder(model, device="cuda")` **does not move the model to the GPU** in
sentence-transformers 3.3.1. The reranker silently ran on CPU at **21.2 s per query** — a
35× penalty — while `torch.cuda.is_available()` returned `True` and every retrieval metric
was identical. An explicit `model.model.to("cuda").half()` fixed it with no change to
R@1, R@5, R@10, MRR or nDCG, confirming that fp16 costs nothing in quality here. Latency
regressions of this kind cannot be caught by an accuracy-only evaluation harness.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Structure-Aware Chunking Outperforms Fixed-Length Chunking on Legal Text

Structure-aware chunking raised mean MRR by 45% over the recursive baseline (0.167 → 0.242)
and lifted the best R@10 from 0.34 to 0.51. This runs **counter to the finding of Qu et al.
(NAACL 2025)**, who report that fixed-size chunking consistently outperforms semantic
chunking.

The contradiction is explicable rather than genuine. Qu et al. evaluate *statistical* semantic
chunking — embedding-similarity breakpoints — on general-domain prose. Our dominant condition
is not statistical: for legislation it parses an explicit, author-provided hierarchy
(`MADDE n`), which is not an estimate of a topic boundary but a declaration of one. The
semantic component applies only to the decision corpus, where no such markup exists. The
proper reading of our result is therefore: **where a document declares its own structure, use
it; statistical segmentation is a fallback, not a default.** Qu et al.'s caution against
paying for inferred boundaries remains intact.

### 6.2 Provenance Context Has an Effect Independent of Boundary Placement

Isolating the provenance prefix (`recursive` → `recursive+prefix`, identical boundaries)
raised mean MRR from 0.167 to 0.200 (+20%). The effect is sharply concentrated:

- BERTurk@75 dense R@1 rose from 0.03 to 0.14 — a 4.7× gain from prepending ~60 characters.
- BM25 recall@5 on referential queries doubled (0.20 → 0.40).

This is a deterministic, LLM-free approximation of Contextual Retrieval, and it reproduces
that method's reported benefit at essentially zero cost. The mechanism is visible in the
query-type split: the prefix is the only place where the article or decision identifier
appears as retrievable text.

**Design consequence.** The lexical index must cover the prefixed text, not the raw body. Our
first implementation indexed `text_raw`, which silently made every referential query
unanswerable by BM25; this was detected only through the query-type disaggregation and is the
strongest argument in this study for reporting retrieval metrics by query class rather than
in aggregate.

### 6.3 A Silently Truncating Encoder

`emrecan/bert-base-turkish-cased-mean-nli-stsb-tr` ships with `max_seq_length = 75` despite a
BERT backbone supporting 512 positions. Measured directly: the embedding of a 1,320-character
chunk and that of its first 300 characters have cosine similarity **1.0000** — the remainder
of the text is discarded with no warning, no exception, and no visible failure. Under
recursive chunking (median 970 characters), roughly three-quarters of every chunk was
invisible to the retriever, producing R@1 = 0.03.

Raising `max_seq_length` to 512 removed the truncation (cosine dropped to 0.8956) but **did
not reliably improve retrieval**. Within the structural+semantic arm it was worse: dense R@10
fell 0.46 → 0.40, hybrid 0.49 → 0.43. Reading longer inputs is not the same as being trained
to represent them; the model was fine-tuned on short NLI/STS pairs, and forcing 512-token
inference moves it outside its training distribution. Mean pooling over many weakly-modelled
tokens dilutes the signal that the first 75 tokens carried.

The practical lesson generalises beyond this model: **`max_seq_length` must be verified
empirically, not inferred from the backbone architecture**, and a truncation defect is
invisible to every standard quality signal — no error, no warning, plausible-looking
neighbours.

### 6.4 Hybrid Fusion Is Conditional, Not Universally Beneficial

Hybrid retrieval has the best marginal mean (0.225 vs. 0.183 dense, 0.201 BM25), but the
aggregate conceals an interaction:

| Dense encoder quality | dense MRR | hybrid MRR | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak (BERTurk@75, structural) | 0.172 | 0.305 | **+77%** |
| Strong (e5-base@512, structural) | 0.330 | 0.292 | **−12%** |

With a fixed fusion constant (RRF, k = 60), BM25 contributes equally regardless of whether the
dense ranking needs help. When the dense retriever is already strong, fusion **injects a
weaker ranking and degrades the result**. The best single configuration by MRR
(`structural+semantic × e5-base × dense`, 0.330) uses no fusion at all; hybrid wins only on
R@10 (0.51), i.e. it broadens recall while blurring precision at the top.

This qualifies the common "hybrid is strictly better" guidance. A defensible production
choice is score-aware or adaptive fusion — weighting BM25 by dense-score confidence, or
routing referential queries (detectable by a decision-number or article-number pattern) to
the lexical index and conceptual queries to the dense index.

### 6.5 The Remaining Gap

The baseline production pipeline without reranking failed on 10 of 35 questions (R@10 = 0.71).
With cross-encoder reranking (`BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3`) and recalibrated authority weighting
($w = 2.0$), recall reached **R@10 = 0.77 (8 misses)** and MRR rose to **0.657** (+73% over
un-reranked hybrid).

The gold set was deliberately constructed around the legal vocabulary gap — *parmak izi*
(fingerprint) against *biyometrik veri* (biometric data), *"kaç gün içinde cevaplamalıyım"*
against *"otuz gün içinde sonuçlandırır"*. The remaining 8 misses reflect cases where neither
the bi-encoder's semantic similarity nor the cross-encoder's token interactions bridge the lexical
chasm without explicit domain knowledge.

RAGTurk (SIGTURK 2026) reports that cross-encoder reranking plus context augmentation attains
84.6% accuracy against a 78.7% baseline at far lower cost than HyDE, and warns that stacking
generative modules distorts morphological cues in Turkish. That points to a specific ordering
of next steps:

1. **Deterministic query expansion** from a curated legal-terminology synonym map (*parmak izi* →
   *biyometrik veri*), which directly attacks the remaining 8 vocabulary misses without a generative
   stage or latency penalty.
2. **Knowledge graph / inventory integration** (Phase 2) — linking regulatory articles to the
   concrete data categories and legal bases in the data inventory.

### 6.6 Threats to Validity

**Gold set size.** 35 questions, of which only 5 are referential, so referential recall moves
in steps of 0.20 and the query-type comparison is indicative rather than conclusive. The
conceptual subset (n = 30) supports the main claims.

**Single annotator.** The gold set was authored by one annotator and verified only for
resolvability against the corpus, not for inter-annotator agreement. Some questions admit
more than one defensible governing provision; we credit only the annotated source, which
understates absolute recall.

**No statistical testing.** Türkel et al. (INTCEC 2026) apply paired McNemar tests under Holm
correction. With 35 questions the present differences — particularly the 0.49 vs. 0.51 gap
between the top configurations — are within plausible sampling noise and should not be
over-read. The chunking effect (+45% mean MRR) is large enough to survive that caveat; the
embedding and fusion effects are reported as directional.

**Retrieval only.** No answer-generation quality is measured. Abualhaija et al. show that
retrieval success and citation groundedness diverge: a system can retrieve the right article
and still cite it incompletely.

### 6.7 Corpus Composition Is a Retrieval Problem, Not Just a Data Problem

Decision summaries are 87% of documents but the second-lowest authority class. Pure similarity
retrieval reflects that imbalance faithfully and therefore wrongly: for *"can I install a
fingerprint reader at the office entrance?"* the unweighted retriever returned ten advisory
summaries and **zero binding provisions**. The generated answer was fluent, correctly
structured, and cited only non-binding sources — the exact groundedness failure Abualhaija et
al. document, arriving through class imbalance rather than model error.

Neither a post-hoc quota nor score weighting fully fixes this. A quota can only promote
candidates that already entered the pool, and Article 6 never did. The effective remedy is a
**separate metadata-filtered retrieval pass** over the binding subset, which is why the
vector store was specified with filter pushdown from the outset. Grounding is thus an
index-design requirement, not a prompt instruction.

A caveat the measurements make visible: the mandatory pass guarantees that *some* binding
source is present, not that it is the *right* one. In the fingerprint example the promoted
provisions came from the Data Protection Expertise Regulation rather than Article 6 — correct
in class, wrong in substance. Closing that gap requires reranking, not retrieval filters.

### 6.8 Adopted Configuration

| Component | Setting | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Chunking | structural (article/paragraph) + semantic for decisions | §6.1, +45% mean MRR |
| Provenance prefix | on, included in both dense and lexical index | §6.2 |
| Embedding | `intfloat/multilingual-e5-base`, 512 tokens, `query:`/`passage:` | §6.3 |
| Retrieval | hybrid RRF (k = 60), dense/lexical top-40 | §6.4 |
| Mandatory pass | top-10, filtered on `baglayicilik LIKE 'zorunlu%'` | §6.7, zero ungrounded |
| Reranker | `BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3`, 80 candidates, min-max normalized | §5.5 |
| Authority weight | w = 2.0 | §5.5, saturation point |
| Returned units | 12 parents (full article or decision) | §4.4 |

End-to-end retrieval quality on the gold set: **R@10 = 0.77, R@5 = 0.71, R@1 = 0.60, MRR = 0.657,
nDCG = 0.684, 8 of 35 questions unretrieved, 0 questions without a binding source.**

`berturk-sts` is not adopted; its default configuration should be regarded as unsafe for
chunks longer than roughly 250 characters.

Hybrid is retained over pure dense despite a lower raw RRF MRR because the answer is generated from
the entire returned set rather than from rank 1, making R@10 and miss count the operative
metrics, and because BM25 is the only component that answers referential queries reliably. The
cross-encoder subsequently restores precision across the top ranks.

### 6.9 Conclusion

Chunking strategy, not embedding model choice, was the dominant factor in this corpus
(+45% vs. +35% mean MRR), and the largest single improvement came from neither: authority
weighting, a domain-specific ranking signal absent from general RAG guidance, contributed
+30% MRR on its own. Cross-encoder reranking over the fused candidate pool further elevated MRR to
0.657 (+73% over un-reranked hybrid) while reducing unretrieved questions from 10 to 8.

The most consequential defects found were silent — an encoder discarding three-quarters of every
chunk with no error surface, and a default sigmoid collapsing cross-encoder logits into near-zero
variance — and were detectable only by direct, component-isolated measurement.

The system retrieves a correct binding source for roughly eight of ten questions (R@10 = 0.77,
MRR = 0.657) and always returns at least one binding provision. That represents a reliable basis
for a compliance assistant. The remaining frontier lies in bridging domain-specific vocabulary gaps
and integrating the structural knowledge graph.
